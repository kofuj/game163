Three of those were Grade C picks at 51.6% — the bottom of the confidence range. All three were right. The Toronto sweep was clean: 5–1, 4–3, 3–0. Then the Rays traveled to Fenway and won 8–4 against a Red Sox team the model rates higher at home.

What the model sees in Tampa Bay: a strong Bayesian posterior built on a better-than-expected first month, a bullpen ERA that ranks in the top quarter of the AL, and a rotation that's been healthy while several other AL teams have been shuffling starters. The 51.6% confidence for the Toronto series wasn't a strong edge — it was the model saying the Rays are slightly better than they're being treated. They've made that case four games in a row.

Tampa Bay is back at Boston today. The model still likes them, at 56.4%.

## Oakland beat Philadelphia 12–1

The model had the Phillies at 55.1% (Grade C). The Athletics won by eleven.

This is the kind of result that looks like a model failure and is actually just baseball. At 55.1%, the model is saying the Phillies have a slight edge — roughly the same confidence it has when it says Tampa Bay might beat Toronto. The underlying numbers for Oakland are not good: their Bayesian win rate is below .500, their Elo rating is near the bottom of the league, and their rotation has been inconsistent. None of that stops them from winning any given game 12–1.
