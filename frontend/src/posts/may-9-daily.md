What makes it worth examining carefully is that the Grade B tier did its job — 3 for 4, with the one miss being Phillies vs. Colorado, which we'll get to — and the Grade C tier produced 27%. The 44% overall tells the story. A coin flip is 50%. The Grade C tier is hitting below coin-flip rate this month. On May 5 and 6, it briefly looked like the tier had found its footing. May 8 reversed that. The Grade C picks on the slate yesterday included eight different teams in eight different matchups, and six of them lost.

This doesn't mean the model's Grade C edges are wrong. It means they are small. At 50–58% confidence, any stretch of 30–40 picks will include weeks that look like May. The model's walk-forward validation shows Grade C accuracy converges toward its expected rate over hundreds of picks, not dozens. What's harder to live with is that in a given month, the Grade C tier can look like noise — because in a given month, it practically is.

## Colorado went to Philadelphia and won 9–7

This is the Grade B miss worth examining. The model had the Phillies at 59% at home against a Colorado team that rates near the bottom of the NL. Colorado is 9th in road ERA and has one of the lower Bayesian win-rate posteriors in the league. Philadelphia plays in a hitter-friendly park but is still meaningfully better than the Rockies by every metric the model uses.

Colorado won 9–7.

A 9-7 game at Citizens Bank Park isn't a fluke — it's a high-scoring game between two offenses in a hitting environment, and the worse team can absolutely win those. The park factor worked both ways: it created the conditions where Colorado's bats stayed in the game long enough to win. The model used the park factor correctly (flagged it as a hitter's park), but a 59% edge doesn't prevent the other team from scoring nine runs on a given night.

The Phillies are back today against Colorado. Same matchup, model picks them again at 58.8% (Grade B).

## The Cubs and Mariners: road favorites doing what they're supposed to

Two Grade B picks won by big margins in situations the model likes: road teams rated higher than the host.

**Cubs 7–1 in Texas.** Chicago has been one of the more consistent teams in the NL this month, with a rotation that's held opponents to fewer than four runs per game over the last ten days. Texas has struggled at home against left-handed starting pitching, which Chicago sent to the mound.

**Seattle 12–8 in Chicago.** The White Sox are one of the worst teams in baseball by Elo and Bayesian rating. Seattle's Bayesian win rate sits above .540, and at home the White Sox have the worst run differential in the AL. The Mariners won by four runs and it wasn't that close.

Both teams are back today in the same matchups.

## Tampa Bay's streak ended — and they're back again

Yesterday's post noted Tampa Bay had won four straight with the model correct each time. They lost 0–2 to Boston in the series finale.

The model still has Tampa Bay at 56.4% against Boston today. Same series, different game, same reasoning: the Rays' underlying numbers haven't changed because of one shutout loss. Their bullpen ERA for the season remains strong and their lineup has been producing runs at a consistent clip. One 0–2 game doesn't move those priors.

Whether Tampa Bay picks up where it left off or extends the losing streak into the new series is exactly the kind of thing a 56.4% pick cannot tell you.
