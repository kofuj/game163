## The Dodgers responded

May 5: Dodgers 1, Astros 2. Grade B miss.

May 6: Dodgers 12, Astros 2. Grade B hit.

The model held the Dodgers at 58.3% across both games. In Game 2 it was wrong by one run. In Game 3 it was right by ten. That variance is not unusual at 58% confidence — the model correctly identified Los Angeles as the better team in a close matchup, and two of the three Houston games validated that read. The one loss was a 1–2 squeaker. The two wins were by a combined 17 runs.

The series is over. The Dodgers don't appear on today's slate.

## Cleveland finally broke the pattern

After losing two straight Grade B picks to Kansas City (6–2, 5–3), the Guardians won Game 3 of that series 3–1. It was a clean result — not a walk-off or a lucky bounce, but a three-run win by the team the model has been backing all week.

What changed from the previous two games is unclear. The Royals' bullpen, which had been excellent, gave up three runs without a lot of drama. The Guardians' Elo rating was right both times it missed and one time it hit, which is roughly what you'd expect from a 58.3% pick in a short series.

The Guardians and Royals are back today. Same pick, same probability: Cleveland 58.3% (Grade B). A fourth game in this series.

## Washington 15, Minnesota 2

The model had Minnesota at 54.8% (Grade C). Minnesota had just won 11–3 over this same Washington team on May 5. On May 6, Washington won 15–2.

This is Grade C variance at its most extreme. A 54.8% pick losing by 13 runs is jarring, but it's not a model failure — it's the natural consequence of making picks at low confidence levels in baseball, where any given game can produce absurd score lines. The model correctly identifies the slight edge. The slight edge does not prevent blowouts.

Minnesota is back on today's slate at 57.4% (Grade C) against Washington again.
