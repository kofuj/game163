Three days in, May has a pattern. Grade B goes 71%, Grade C goes 25%, and the headline number sits in the mid-to-high 40s and looks bad. The underlying model is doing exactly what it should. The problem is that six out of ten games every day sit in the 50–58% band where the model's edge is too thin to survive variance at full speed.

The two numbers that matter: Grade B is 14 for 20 across the first three days of May. Grade C is 7 for 25.

---

## The Dodgers close out St. Louis

Four games. Three losses, one win. Final score on Sunday: 4–1, Dodgers.

The model called Los Angeles in all four games at 62–65%. They went 1–3. The prior held and it turned out to be right exactly once — at the end. That is what Bayesian persistence looks like when it encounters a hot opponent: it does not move, it absorbs the punishment, and then when the opponent cools it is still standing.

The Cardinals won their series 3–1. Los Angeles took the finale convincingly — four runs on a comfortable pitching performance — and left Busch Stadium with the streak behind them. The model will now reset the Cardinals' home-series context; three wins over the Dodgers generated a meaningful update to St. Louis's Bayesian posterior, and their current win rate now reflects a team playing well at home in early May.
