Three days. Three different personalities.

May 3 looked bad in the headline — 46.7% — but Grade B went 5 for 7, and the Dodgers finally beat the Cardinals. May 4 was a flat 50%, with Grade B going 3 for 4 but Grade C producing almost nothing. May 5 was the best day of the three at 61.5%, driven by the Grade C tier for once actually holding up. Across all three days, Grade B sits at 67%. Grade C sits at 44%. The pattern is consistent.

## The Cardinals situation resolved

After three straight losses in St. Louis, the Dodgers won Game 4 of that series 4–1 on May 3. The final-game margin was decisive — not a lucky one-run win but a clean four-run result that matched the model's conviction. The model's 64.8% estimate held across four games, and it was right three times. The Cardinals simply had an excellent homestand.

What followed is arguably more interesting. The Dodgers moved to Houston, and the model immediately liked them again — 58.3% both days. Game 1 in Houston: Dodgers 8, Astros 3. A clean hit. Game 2: Dodgers 1, Astros 2. A one-run loss in what the model treated as a virtual coin flip toward Los Angeles. That is a 3–1 record for the model on Dodgers picks over the last four days, against two different opponents. The Cardinals series was the outlier; it has not repeated.

The Dodgers appear again today at 58.3%. Game 3 in Houston.

## Cleveland and Kansas City

This is the pattern that warrants attention now.

**May 4:** Cleveland Guardians @ Kansas City Royals. Model picks Cleveland at 58.3% (Grade B). Final: Royals 6, Guardians 2.

**May 5:** Same series, same pick. Model picks Cleveland at 58.3% (Grade B). Final: Royals 5, Guardians 3.

Two consecutive Grade B misses against the same opponent. The Guardians have a higher Elo rating, a stronger Bayesian posterior, and a better run differential over the season. Kansas City has simply been better in this series — both games were multi-run Royal wins, not close calls.

The model does not hard-code series momentum. Three games against one opponent won't meaningfully move a team's season-long ratings. What it doesn't yet see: the Royals' bullpen has been excellent over the last two weeks, posting one of the lowest ERAs in the AL, and their home offense has been hitting .290 with runners in scoring position this month. These are the same kind of situational factors that made the Cardinals dangerous at home. They are real, but they are also the kind that dissolve over a larger sample — which is why the model doesn't weight them heavily.

For now, Cleveland and Kansas City do not play each other today. The Guardians return to the slate against KC tomorrow. Worth watching.
