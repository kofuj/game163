## The Yankees got swept in Milwaukee

Three games. Three losses. The model backed New York every time.

| Game | Score | Pick | Prob | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| May 8 | Brewers 6, Yankees 0 | Yankees | 52.2% | MISS |
| May 9 | Brewers 4, Yankees 3 | Yankees | 52.8% | MISS |
| May 10 | Brewers 4, Yankees 3 | Yankees | 54.9% | MISS |

All three were Grade C picks. The model's estimate actually climbed across the series — from 52.2% to 54.9% — as the Yankees' broader numbers look better than the Milwaukee results reflect. That's how Bayesian updating works with a small sample: three losses to one opponent aren't enough to significantly revise a team's rating when hundreds of games of evidence point the other way.

What the model doesn't see cleanly: Milwaukee's home bullpen has been the best in the NL this month by ERA, and the Brewers have been particularly effective stranding runners in the middle innings. The Yankees' offense has generated runs in this series — they scored 3 in Games 2 and 3 — but haven't been able to close out close games on the road.

The series is over. The Yankees move to Baltimore today, where the model backs them again at 59.0%.

## Grade B's worst day: the series trifecta

Three of May 10's four Grade B picks were series Game 3s the model had called correctly in Game 1 and incorrectly in Game 2. All three lost.

| Series | Game 1 | Game 2 | Game 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cubs @ Rangers | HIT (7–1) | MISS (0–6) | MISS (0–3) |
| Mariners @ White Sox | HIT (12–8) | MISS (1–6) | MISS (1–2) |
| Blue Jays vs Angels | HIT (14–1) | MISS (1–0 Angels) | MISS (1–6 Angels) |

Each series ended 2-1 in favor of the team the model was betting against. The White Sox, Rangers, and Angels — three of the lower-rated teams on the model's board — all won their series against higher-rated opponents.

This is not a failure of the Grade B edge so much as an illustration of how series work in baseball. A 58-59% pick winning Game 1 gives no information about Game 2 or 3. The edge is per game, not per series. Over three games with a true probability of 58%, you'd expect to win all three about 20% of the time and go 1-2 or worse about 40% of the time. This week landed in that 40%.

The fourth Grade B pick — Phillies over Colorado at 58.7% — won 6-0, matching the model's confidence cleanly.
